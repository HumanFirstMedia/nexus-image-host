Guide for Brannon

How to use SPORT before anything goes live.

This is the practical version. Open the asset, run the five-letter check, rewrite the weak spots, then publish only when the piece feels specific to Fit Buddha.

The 7-minute review

  1. Read the asset out loud.
  2. Circle every vague wellness phrase.
  3. Run SPORT from S to T.
  4. Rewrite the first headline and CTA first.
  5. Check that the intro offer appears early.
  6. Make community physical, not decorative.
  7. Approve only when the piece sounds local, athletic, and human.
01 / the framework

SPORT means the asset has to hold five jobs.

The brand guide is the standard. SPORT is the operating move. It turns the standard into a review tool Brannon can use on pages, captions, front desk scripts, instructor notes, emails, ads, and campaign briefs.

S

Studio truth

Does this name Lagree, Megaformer, 40 minutes, strength, endurance, posture, core, or low-impact intensity?

P

People

Does this show the actual humans in the room - coaches, beginners, regulars, modifications, names, care?

O

Offer

Is the next step obvious? The default entry point is “Try 2 classes for $35.”

R

Retention

Does the message explain why they come back after the intro classes?

T

Tone

Is it strong, warm, local, and grounded - without mystical fog or generic fitness hype?

02 / approval checklist

Brannon’s pass-fail review.

Use this before approving a homepage section, social post, flyer, ad, or email. One “no” does not kill the asset. It tells you exactly where to fix it.

Can a new person understand the workout in ten seconds?

If not, add Megaformer, Lagree, 40 minutes, low impact, high intensity, expert instruction.

Does the copy lower the intimidation barrier?

Beginner-friendly does not mean easy. It means coached, modified, and supported.

Is the community promise behavioral?

Say what people do: greet you, remember your name, cue your form, celebrate your milestones.

Is the offer visible before the scroll gets heavy?

Do not bury the first yes. Repeat it after the strongest proof section.

Could this copy belong to any wellness studio?

If yes, it is not ready. Add Fit Buddha specifics, local proof, and real class language.

03 / rewrite examples

Before and after.

SPORT does not make the brand colder. It makes the warmth easier to believe because the promise is attached to real class experience.

Before - too vague

  • “Transform your body, mind, and spirit.”
  • “Awaken your inner strength.”
  • “Join our holistic wellness community.”
  • “Experience integrated fitness.”

After - SPORT ready

  • “40 minutes on the Megaformer. Full-body strength without wrecking your joints.”
  • “Hard class. Warm room. Coaches who actually see you.”
  • “Try 2 classes for $35 in Santa Barbara or Ventura.”
  • “Come for the burn. Stay because they know you.”

Brannon’s quick script to the team.

Use this language when handing SPORT to a designer, copywriter, front desk lead, or instructor who needs to understand the new direction quickly.

“Before this goes live, run SPORT. I want the workout clear, the room human, the intro offer visible, the return reason obvious, and the tone grounded. If it sounds like a generic wellness brand, bring it back to the Megaformer and the people in the room.”

The rule is simple: athletic first, community second, transformation earned.

If the asset follows that order, it will feel more trustworthy and sell the first class faster.
